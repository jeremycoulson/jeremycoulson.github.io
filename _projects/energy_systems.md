Modern power systems are becoming increasingly complex due to the integration of renewable energy sources, power electronics, and distributed generation. Traditional model-based control approaches struggle to keep pace with this complexity, motivating the use of data-driven methods that can adapt to changing system conditions without requiring detailed first-principles models.

Our research has applied data-enabled predictive control to grid-connected power converters and power system oscillation damping. We have shown that DeePC can effectively regulate power converter outputs and damp inter-area oscillations in power networks using only measured data, without requiring knowledge of the network topology or detailed component models. We have also developed decentralized data-driven control architectures that enable scalable control of large-scale power systems.

Key papers include data-enabled predictive control for grid-connected power converters (CDC 2019) and decentralized data-enabled predictive control for power system oscillation damping (IEEE TCST 2021).
